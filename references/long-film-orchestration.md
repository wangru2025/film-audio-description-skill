# Feature-length orchestration

## Pre-scan before delegation

Inspect the entire media, create the transcript/VAD timeline, detect scene boundaries, and make a low-frequency visual overview before dividing work. Split on scene or chapter boundaries, normally into 8–15 minute analysis segments with 15–30 seconds of context overlap. Do not cut an important dialogue or action chain merely to meet a fixed duration.

## Roles

The coordinating agent owns project state, canonical names, reveal policy, segmentation, merged script, approvals, synthesis, mix, and QC. Segment agents provide structured observations and candidate cues only. When useful, assign a separate audio/speaker pass and a film-wide continuity audit.

With limited concurrency, process segments in waves. Never spawn more agents than available slots. Each agent writes only inside its assigned segment directory; do not let parallel agents edit the same bible or merged script.

## Segment work package

Provide boundaries, overlapping context, transcript, contact sheets or clips, current character/location/object bible, reveal restrictions, user preferences, and output schema. Ask for observations including subject, action, object, location, importance, soundtrack sufficiency, confidence, uncertainty, and candidate cue.

Overlap gives context but not duplicate output. The coordinator resolves overlapping observations and chooses one cue or none.

## Iterative review

For work over 30 minutes, default to approximately 20-minute review batches. Apply corrections to the bible and instructions for later waves. Approved batches are stable, but the final film-wide pass may propose changes; any meaning-changing merged revision requires final approval.

## Film-wide audit

Check aliases, repeated first introductions, cross-segment cause and effect, object continuity, location clarity, reveal order, density drift, duplicated overlap cues, contradictory observations, and unresolved low-confidence items. Supply the auditor the merged artifacts rather than asking it to re-watch the whole film from scratch.
